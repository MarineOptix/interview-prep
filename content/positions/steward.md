## Q: What are your daily duties as a Steward?

I work under the Cook, and my daily duties are:

- **Mess rooms**: preparing the tables, serving meals and cleaning after each meal.
- **Galley support**: helping the Cook with food preparation, washing dishes and keeping the galley clean.
- **Accommodation**: cleaning the officers' cabins as required by the company, alleyways, toilets, the hospital and public rooms.
- **Laundry**: washing bed linen and towels and keeping the linen locker in order.
- **Stores**: helping receive and stow provisions and keeping the storerooms clean.

I organise my day around meal times, so the mess rooms are always ready on time. I also follow my duties on the muster list in emergencies and take part in all drills.

## Q: How do you use cleaning chemicals safely?

Before I use a chemical, I know what it is and how to use it. Every chemical has a safety data sheet, and I follow the instructions for dilution and the PPE needed: usually gloves, and goggles for strong products.

- I **never mix chemicals**, especially bleach with acid-based cleaners, because they can produce toxic gas.
- I keep chemicals in their **original labelled containers**, never in food or drink containers.
- I store them **away from food**, in the chemical store, secured for sea.
- I use them in a **well-ventilated** area.

If a chemical gets on my skin or in my eyes, I rinse immediately with plenty of water and report it. Using the right amount of chemical is also important: more is not cleaner, it is only more dangerous and more expensive.

## Q: How do you keep the galley and mess rooms hygienic?

I follow a cleaning schedule, daily and weekly, set by the Cook and the company:

- **After every meal**: tables, chairs and serving areas cleaned and disinfected; dishes washed at the right temperature and dried.
- **Daily**: floors, sinks, work surfaces and waste bins cleaned; fridge handles and door handles disinfected.
- **Weekly**: deep cleaning of fridges, storerooms and areas behind equipment, and cleaning of extraction filters if assigned.

I keep my personal hygiene high: clean uniform, washed hands, hair covered when helping in the galley, and I report any illness, especially stomach problems, before working with food. Pests like cockroaches are reported immediately so they can be treated.

## Q: How do you deal with garbage on board?

Garbage is managed under MARPOL Annex V and the ship's Garbage Management Plan. My part is to separate the garbage correctly at the source, in the colour-coded bins:

- **Plastics**: never thrown overboard; kept on board for disposal ashore or incineration if allowed.
- **Food waste**: kept separate, because its disposal depends on the distance from land and the area.
- **Domestic waste**, paper, glass, cans and **cooking oil**: each in the right container.
- **Hazardous waste**, such as batteries, light bulbs and aerosol cans, separate.

I make sure no garbage goes overboard by mistake, for example with wind on deck, and I report the quantities to the officer who keeps the Garbage Record Book. Mistakes in garbage handling can cause fines in port, so I take it seriously.

## Q: What are your duties in an emergency?

My duties are on the muster list and my cabin card, and I learn them during familiarisation on the first day. For a Steward, the typical duties are:

- **Fire**: going to the muster station, then tasks such as carrying first aid equipment or helping with boundary cooling, as assigned.
- **Abandon ship**: bringing extra blankets, provisions or water to the lifeboat if assigned, and helping crew and any passengers put on lifejackets.
- **Medical emergencies**: assisting the officer responsible for medical care with the stretcher or first aid.

I also know the escape routes from the accommodation, galley and laundry, and the location of the nearest fire extinguishers and alarm buttons. In drills I take my role seriously, because in a real emergency there is no time to learn.
