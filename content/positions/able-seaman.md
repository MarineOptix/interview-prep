## Q: How do you respond to helm orders and change over to hand steering?

I repeat every helm order back clearly, carry it out, and report when it is done. For example: the officer orders "Starboard ten", I answer "Starboard ten", put the wheel to starboard ten degrees and report "Wheel is starboard ten". When given a course: "Steer one-two-five", I answer "Steer one-two-five" and report "Steady on one-two-five" when the ship is on course.

To change over from autopilot to hand steering, I do it only on the order of the officer of the watch. I check the heading, put the wheel amidships and switch the steering mode selector to hand steering according to the bridge procedure, then confirm the ship answers the helm and report "Hand steering, course one-two-five".

I also know how to change to emergency steering from the steering gear room, because we practise it in steering gear drills.

## Q: What are your duties on gangway watch in port?

The gangway watch is the first line of the ship's security under the ISPS Code. My duties are:

- **Access control**: check the identity of every visitor against the expected list, record name, company, time in and out in the visitor log, and issue visitor passes. No one comes on board without permission.
- **Searches**: check bags and stores according to the security level and the ship's security plan.
- **Gangway safety**: adjust the gangway with the tide and draft, check the safety net, lighting and the lifebuoy with line.
- **Moorings**: watch the mooring lines and tend them when needed.
- **Report**: anything suspicious, unauthorised persons, small boats near the ship, or pollution to the officer of the watch.

If the security level changes, I follow the extra measures given by the Ship Security Officer. I stay at the gangway until I am properly relieved.

## Q: How do you rig a pilot ladder?

I rig it according to SOLAS regulation V/23 and the company procedure, under the supervision of the responsible officer:

- The ladder must be in **good condition**, certified, with no damaged steps or ropes; I check it before each use.
- It is rigged so that the pilot climbs **not less than 1.5 m and not more than 9 m**; if the climb would be more than 9 m, the accommodation ladder is used together with the pilot ladder.
- The ladder rests firmly against the ship's side, clear of discharges, and **secured to strong points** on deck, not to the rails.
- The steps are **horizontal** and the bottom is at the height the pilot asks for.
- At the top there are **handholds**, a lifebuoy with a self-igniting light, a heaving line and good lighting at night.

An officer and a crew member with a radio stay at the ladder while the pilot boards or leaves. I never leave the ladder unattended during the operation.

## Q: What is a good lookout?

A good lookout uses sight and hearing all the time to find anything that could affect the ship's safety: other vessels, lights, buoys, small boats, floating objects, changes in the weather or sound signals.

I take position where I can see well and I am not doing any other work that distracts me. I scan the whole horizon in a regular pattern, not only ahead, and I let my eyes adjust to darkness at night.

I report everything I see to the officer of the watch with a clear report: what, where (relative bearing) and approximate distance. If the bearing of a ship stays the same, I tell the officer, because that may mean risk of collision.

I do not assume that the officer has already seen a target on radar. Reporting something twice is better than not reporting it at all.

## Q: What are your duties in an abandon ship drill?

My duties are written on the muster list and my cabin card, and I learn them during familiarisation. When the abandon ship signal is given on the Master's order, I:

1. Go to my muster station with my lifejacket, warm clothes and immersion suit if required.
2. Report to the officer in charge for the headcount.
3. Carry out my duty at the lifeboat, for example preparing the boat, removing the lashings, checking the drain plug, preparing the painter, or operating the winch under the officer's orders.
4. Help the crew board the lifeboat in order and check that everyone wears the lifejacket correctly.

As an AB, I may also be one of the persons in charge of the boat's engine or the launching, so I need to know how to start the engine and how the release gear works. Drills are the time to ask questions and practise, not to stand and watch.
