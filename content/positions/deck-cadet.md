## Q: Why do you want to work at sea?

I want a career where I take real responsibility early and keep learning every day. At sea, a young officer is responsible for the safety of the ship, the crew and the cargo during the watch, and I find that motivating rather than frightening.

I chose the deck side because I enjoy navigation and the practical side of seamanship: [add what got you interested — a family member at sea, training ship practice, a subject you liked at the academy].

My plan is to complete my sea service and training record book with good quality, pass my certificate of competency as officer of the watch, and grow with one company. I know the life has difficult parts: long months away from home, hard work and living in a small community. I am ready for that, and I think the cadetship is the time to prove it.

## Q: What do you expect to do on board as a cadet?

My main goal is to learn and to complete the tasks in my training record book, under the supervision of the officers. I expect to:

- Keep **bridge watches** with the officer of the watch: lookout, position fixing, chart work, radar and ECDIS, and logbook entries.
- Work on **deck** with the Bosun and the crew: mooring, maintenance, painting and cargo operations, so I understand the work I will later supervise.
- Help with **safety equipment** checks and take an active part in drills.
- Assist the officers during **cargo operations** and learn the ship's stability and loading computer.

I also expect to do some cleaning and routine jobs, and that is fine. I would ask the officers to sign my record book regularly rather than at the end of the contract, so there is time to repeat anything I have not learned properly.

## Q: You hear the general emergency alarm. What do you do?

The general emergency alarm is seven or more short blasts followed by one long blast on the ship's whistle and the alarm bells. When I hear it, I:

1. Put on warm clothing and take my lifejacket and, if required by the muster list, my immersion suit.
2. Go directly to my muster station by the shortest safe route, using the escape route if the normal one is blocked.
3. Report to the person in charge so the crew can be counted.
4. Carry out the duties written for me on the muster list and my cabin card, and follow the orders of the officer in charge.

As a new crew member, I learn my muster station and duties during familiarisation on the first day, before the ship sails. I also learn the different alarm signals on that ship, because fire or abandon ship signals can differ between companies.

## Q: How do you report a target when you are on lookout?

A report should be short, clear and complete, so the officer of the watch can act on it immediately. I report:

- **What** I see: a ship, a light, a buoy, a small boat, an object in the water.
- **Where** it is: the relative bearing, for example "fine on the starboard bow", "two points on the port bow" or in degrees relative to the ship's head, and the approximate distance.
- **What it is doing**, if I can tell: lights showing, the aspect or direction of movement.

For example: "Ship's light, two points on the starboard bow, about five miles, showing a green light." I keep watching the target and report again if its bearing does not change, because that can mean risk of collision.

I also report anything unusual, such as a change in weather, smoke or a sound signal. If I am not sure what I see, I still report it.

## Q: What will you do if you do not understand an instruction from an officer?

I ask immediately. A misunderstood order can be dangerous, especially on deck during mooring or in the engine room, and asking is always better than guessing.

I repeat the instruction back in my own words, for example "You want me to close valve number three on the starboard side, is that correct?" This is called closed-loop communication, and it catches mistakes before they happen.

If English is not the first language for me or for the officer, I speak slowly and simply and use standard marine communication phrases where possible. If a task is new to me, I ask to be shown how to do it the first time.

I also think it is important to say when I have finished a task or when I have a problem, instead of waiting for someone to check on me.
