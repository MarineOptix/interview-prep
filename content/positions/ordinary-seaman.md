## Q: Which knots do you know and where are they used?

The most important knots I use on deck are:

- **Bowline**: makes a loop that does not slip or jam; used to make a temporary eye in a line or as a rescue loop around a person.
- **Clove hitch**: quickly fastens a line to a rail or a pole.
- **Round turn and two half hitches**: secures a line to a ring or a post and holds under load.
- **Reef knot**: joins two ropes of the same size, used for light work such as tying bundles; it is not a safe knot for heavy loads.
- **Sheet bend**: joins two ropes of different sizes.
- **Figure-of-eight**: a stopper knot at the end of a line so it does not run out through a block.

I also know how to make up and stow lines, whip a rope end and do a basic eye splice.

## Q: What precautions do you take when chipping and painting?

Before I start, I make sure the Bosun has explained the job and the hazards. For chipping and painting I use:

- **Eye protection**: goggles or a face shield, because rust and paint flakes fly.
- **Ear protection** when using chipping hammers or needle guns.
- **Gloves, coveralls and safety shoes**, and a dust mask or respirator according to the job and the product.

I read the safety data sheet for the paint or solvent, or ask the Bosun about the hazards. I work in well-ventilated areas and never use paint or solvents in enclosed spaces without a permit and ventilation.

I keep the work area tidy, clean up spills, and close paint tins after use. Oily rags and empty tins go to the right garbage containers, because they are a fire hazard. I do not work near hot work or under people working aloft.

## Q: You discover a fire on board. What do you do?

1. **Raise the alarm** immediately: shout "Fire!", press the nearest fire alarm push button, and inform the bridge by phone or radio with the location of the fire.
2. **Try to fight a small fire** with the right portable extinguisher only if it is safe and I have an escape route behind me.
3. **Limit the fire**: close doors and openings to reduce oxygen, if it is safe to do so.
4. **Go to my muster station** and report to the officer in charge, then carry out my duties on the muster list.

I never put myself in danger to fight a fire that is too big, and I never use water on an electrical fire or burning oil. I know the location of extinguishers, fire hoses and alarm push buttons on my working areas, and I learned them during familiarisation.

## Q: How do you make sure you get enough rest on board?

Rest is a safety issue. Under STCW and MLC 2006, I must have at least 10 hours of rest in any 24-hour period and 77 hours in any 7-day period. The ship keeps records of rest hours, and I make sure my records are true.

In practice, I use my rest time to sleep and not stay up late, especially before a watch or a mooring operation. I eat properly and keep in touch with my family when there is internet, without losing sleep over it.

If I am too tired to work safely, I tell the Bosun or the officer. Fatigue causes accidents, and a good company prefers a crew member who reports it.

I also respect the rest of others: I keep noise low in the accommodation and I am on time to relieve my colleagues.
