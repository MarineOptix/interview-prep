# Crewing interviews — general reference

General reference only. Facts about the candidate come exclusively from their resume.

## How crewing interviews usually run
- Conducted in English by a crewing manager, superintendent or senior officer, in person or by video; often 30 to 60 minutes.
- Typical flow: introduction and documents check, sea service and experience, technical and regulatory knowledge, safety scenarios, behavioural questions, contract and closing questions.
- Some companies add a written or computer-based test (e.g. Marlins English test, CES competence tests).

## “Tell me about yourself”
- 60 to 90 seconds: current rank and total sea service, vessel types and sizes, one or two strongest points relevant to the vacancy, why this position now. Not a life story.

## Motivation answers
- Link the candidate’s real experience to the vessel type, company or trading area of the vacancy.
- Show interest in long-term cooperation and professional growth (next rank, new vessel type, additional certificates) rather than only salary.

## Experience answers
- Be specific: vessel types, sizes, equipment, typical operations, number of contracts.
- Use real numbers and facts from the resume only; when a detail is missing, give a placeholder for the candidate to fill in.

## Weak points and gaps
- Be honest about experience gaps (e.g. first time on this vessel type) and show how they will be closed: familiarisation, company training, previous transferable experience.

## Salary and contract
- Know the market for the rank and vessel type; it is acceptable to state an expectation calmly or say the candidate is open to the company’s scale for the rank.
- Confirm availability date, contract length and rotation, and readiness for travel.

## Questions the candidate can ask
- Vessel details (type, age, main equipment), crew composition and nationalities, contract length and rotation, joining port and date, training and promotion opportunities, company fleet plans.

## Documents usually checked
- Passport, seaman’s book, CoC and endorsements, STCW certificates, medical certificate, visas (e.g. US C1/D), references / sea service letters, vaccination records.
