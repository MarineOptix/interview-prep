# Maritime conventions — reference for interview answers

General reference only. Facts about the candidate come exclusively from their resume.

## STCW (International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers, 1978, as amended)
- Major revisions: 1995 and the 2010 Manila Amendments (in force 1 January 2012).
- Sets minimum training, certification and watchkeeping standards. The STCW Code has Part A (mandatory) and Part B (recommended guidance).
- Chapter II — master and deck department; Chapter III — engine department; Chapter IV — radiocommunication (GMDSS); Chapter V — special training for certain ship types (tankers, passenger ships, IGF and Polar Code ships); Chapter VI — emergency, occupational safety, security, medical care and survival functions; Chapter VIII — watchkeeping.
- Levels of responsibility: management level (Master, Chief Officer, Chief Engineer, Second Engineer), operational level (officers in charge of a navigational or engineering watch), support level (ratings).
- Basic safety training (personal survival techniques, fire prevention and fire fighting, elementary first aid, personal safety and social responsibilities) needs refresher training to prove continued competence every five years for the PST and FPFF elements.
- Hours of rest (STCW A-VIII/1): at least 10 hours in any 24-hour period and 77 hours in any 7-day period; the 10 hours may be split into no more than two periods, one at least 6 hours long, and the interval between consecutive rest periods must not exceed 14 hours. Records of rest hours must be kept.
- Alcohol limit for watchkeeping duties: no more than 0.05% blood alcohol content or 0.25 mg/l in breath.

## SOLAS (International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea, 1974, as amended)
- Chapter II-1 construction, subdivision and stability, machinery and electrical installations; II-2 fire protection, detection and extinction; III life-saving appliances and arrangements; IV radiocommunications; V safety of navigation; VI carriage of cargoes; VII dangerous goods; IX ISM Code; XI-2 maritime security (ISPS Code).
- Abandon ship drill and fire drill: each crew member takes part in at least one of each every month; if more than 25% of the crew have not taken part in drills on board that ship in the previous month, drills are held within 24 hours of leaving port.
- Each lifeboat is launched with its assigned operating crew and manoeuvred in the water at least once every three months during an abandon ship drill.
- Enclosed space entry and rescue drills at least every two months (SOLAS III/19).
- The muster list shows each crew member’s duties in an emergency and is posted in conspicuous places.

## MARPOL (International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships, 1973/78)
- Annex I oil (Oil Record Book Parts I and II, oily water separator, 15 ppm oil content limit for discharge of machinery space bilge water through approved equipment while en route, with further restrictions in special areas, SOPEP).
- Annex II noxious liquid substances in bulk (Cargo Record Book, P&A Manual).
- Annex III harmful substances in packaged form.
- Annex IV sewage.
- Annex V garbage (Garbage Record Book, garbage management plan; discharge of plastics into the sea is prohibited everywhere).
- Annex VI air pollution: sulphur limit 0.50% m/m globally since 1 January 2020 and 0.10% in Emission Control Areas; NOx Tier limits; energy efficiency (EEDI, EEXI, CII, SEEMP).
- Record books must be kept on board and are inspected during Port State Control.

## ISM Code (International Safety Management Code, SOLAS Chapter IX)
- Requires the company to establish a Safety Management System (SMS): safety and environmental policy, procedures, reporting of accidents and non-conformities, emergency preparedness, maintenance, documentation, verification and review.
- The Designated Person Ashore (DPA) is the link between ship and highest level of management.
- Company holds a Document of Compliance (DOC); each ship holds a Safety Management Certificate (SMC).
- Master’s overriding authority and responsibility to make decisions with respect to safety and pollution prevention.
- Near-miss and non-conformity reporting is a core part of continuous improvement.

## ISPS Code (International Ship and Port Facility Security Code, SOLAS Chapter XI-2)
- Security levels 1 (normal), 2 (heightened), 3 (exceptional).
- Roles: Ship Security Officer (SSO), Company Security Officer (CSO), Port Facility Security Officer (PFSO).
- Ship Security Plan (SSP) is approved by the flag Administration; Ship Security Alert System (SSAS); International Ship Security Certificate (ISSC).
- Typical measures: access control, gangway watch and visitor log, restricted areas, monitoring, handling of stores and baggage, Declaration of Security when required.

## MLC, 2006 (Maritime Labour Convention)
- Often called the “seafarers’ bill of rights”. Covers minimum age (16; 18 for night work and hazardous work), medical certificate, qualifications, seafarer employment agreement, wages, hours of work and rest, leave, repatriation, accommodation, food and catering, medical care, on-board and onshore complaint procedures.
- Hours of work limits: maximum 14 hours in any 24-hour period and 72 hours in any 7-day period; or hours of rest: minimum 10 hours in any 24-hour period and 77 hours in any 7-day period.
- Maximum continuous period on board before repatriation entitlement is less than 12 months.
- Ships of 500 GT and above on international voyages carry a Maritime Labour Certificate and a Declaration of Maritime Labour Compliance (DMLC).

## Other frequently mentioned instruments
- COLREGs 1972 — rules for preventing collisions at sea (look-out Rule 5, safe speed Rule 6, risk of collision Rule 7, action to avoid collision Rule 8, crossing situation Rule 15, give-way vessel Rule 16, stand-on vessel Rule 17, restricted visibility Rule 19).
- Load Lines Convention 1966; Ballast Water Management Convention 2004 (D-2 standard, Ballast Water Record Book); Polar Code; IMDG Code (dangerous goods); IMSBC Code (solid bulk cargoes); IBC and IGC Codes (chemical and gas carriers).
- Port State Control: inspections by the port state under regional MoUs (Paris MoU, Tokyo MoU and others); detentions for serious deficiencies.
- Vetting and inspections on tankers: SIRE 2.0 (OCIMF), CDI for chemical tankers; company TMSA.
