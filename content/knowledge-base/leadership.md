# Teamwork, leadership and communication — reference for interview answers

General reference only. Facts about the candidate come exclusively from their resume.

## Answer structure for behavioural questions
- STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the situation short; spend most of the answer on what the candidate personally did and the outcome; finish with what they learned.
- If the resume does not contain a suitable real example, the answer must leave a placeholder for the candidate to fill in with their own story instead of inventing one.

## Multinational crews
- Common working language on board is English (SMCP — IMO Standard Marine Communication Phrases for critical communication).
- Respect for different cultures and religions, clear and simple instructions, closed-loop communication (repeat back), checking understanding rather than assuming.

## Bridge / Engine Resource Management
- Situational awareness, challenge and response, avoiding a single point of failure, effective use of all resources, speaking up regardless of rank.
- Briefings before critical operations (pilotage, cargo, bunkering, maintenance on critical equipment) and debriefings after.

## Supervising and training juniors
- Give clear tasks with the reason behind them, check the work, give feedback, delegate according to competence, familiarisation for new joiners, mentoring cadets (training record book).

## Conflict on board
- Address early and privately, listen to both sides, focus on the work issue not the person, involve the head of department or Master if needed, follow company procedures, document if serious.

## Fatigue and wellbeing
- Respect rest hours, report fatigue honestly, plan work around watches, look out for colleagues (isolation, stress), use welfare facilities, company mental health resources.

## Communication with shore
- Accurate and timely reports to the office, superintendents, charterers and agents; escalate problems early.
