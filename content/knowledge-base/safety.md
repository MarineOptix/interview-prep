# Safety and emergency response — reference for interview answers

General reference only. Facts about the candidate come exclusively from their resume.

## General emergency response
- Raise the alarm, inform the bridge / OOW and Master, follow the muster list and the company emergency procedures from the SMS.
- Account for all personnel at muster; communicate clearly with the on-scene commander.
- After the event: debriefing, report under the SMS, lessons learned shared with the crew.

## Fire
- Fire triangle / tetrahedron: heat, fuel, oxygen, chain reaction.
- Classes and agents (common company practice): A solids — water, foam; B flammable liquids — foam, dry powder, CO2; C gases — isolate the source, dry powder; electrical fires — isolate power, CO2 or dry powder; F/K cooking oils — wet chemical, fire blanket.
- Actions: raise the alarm, isolate (ventilation, fuel, electrical), boundary cooling, attack with the correct team in breathing apparatus, keep the bridge informed.
- Fixed CO2 flooding of the engine room: only on Master’s order after confirming everyone has left and the space is sealed; do not re-enter early.
- Galley fires: switch off heat source and extraction fan, never use water on burning oil.

## Man overboard
- Shout “Man overboard”, throw a lifebuoy with light and smoke signal, keep the person in sight, inform the bridge.
- Bridge: release MOB marker, press MOB on GPS/ECDIS, sound the alarm, start a recovery manoeuvre (Anderson turn for immediate action; Williamson turn for delayed action or poor visibility, it brings the ship back onto its own track; Scharnow turn when the person has been missing for some time, not suitable for immediate action), post look-outs, prepare the rescue boat, send urgency or distress messages as appropriate.

## Abandon ship
- Abandon ship only on the Master’s order. The general emergency alarm is seven or more short blasts followed by one long blast; muster with lifejacket and warm clothing.
- Launch survival craft per the muster list; take EPIRB and SART; stay together, maintain watch, ration water.

## Enclosed space entry
- Permit to work, risk assessment, isolation of the space, ventilation, gas testing (oxygen 21% by volume — 20.9% on most meters, flammable gas below 1% LFL, toxic gases below occupational exposure limits) from top, middle and bottom, standby person at the entrance with communication, rescue equipment ready.
- Never enter to rescue without breathing apparatus; most enclosed space fatalities are would-be rescuers.

## Safe working practices
- Permit-to-work systems (hot work, working aloft, working over the side, electrical isolation / lock-out tag-out).
- Toolbox talks before jobs, PPE, Job Hazard Analysis / risk assessment, Stop Work Authority — every crew member may stop an unsafe job.
- Near-miss reporting without blame; safety committee meetings on board.
- Mooring operations: snap-back zones, never stand in the bight of a line, communication between bridge and mooring stations.

## Drills
- Fire and abandon ship drills at least monthly for every crew member (SOLAS III/19); enclosed space rescue drills every two months; lifeboat launched and manoeuvred every three months.
- Other drills from the SMS: oil spill (SOPEP), steering gear failure, collision, grounding, flooding, security drills (ISPS, at least every three months).

## Medical
- Medical chest, Medical Care certificate holders, radio medical advice (TMAS); record treatment in the medical log.
